What is the Greek test for cancer?

The Greek test is a liquid biopsy from RGCC, a lab in Greece. A blood sample is shipped overseas, and the lab reports how the cancer cells responded to chemotherapy drugs and natural substances, and which genes in the cancer DNA are active. Dr. Trisha Wimbs uses it to guide integrative care.

Is the RGCC test covered by insurance, and what does it cost?

No. Dr. Wimbs says the test is about $3,500, and the first three months of integrative care run about $5,000 to $8,000 if a person does everything recommended. She notes some Christian health share plans may cover integrative care for people who join before a diagnosis.

Can I do integrative cancer care alongside chemo?

Dr. Wimbs says yes, and that integrative oncology does not mean skipping chemo. Her team reviews what a person has already been through, then adds support such as vitamin C and ozone infusions timed 48 hours or more before chemo. Tell your oncologist about anything you add.

Are antioxidants safe during chemotherapy?

Not always. Dr. Wimbs tells people in cancer treatment to avoid glutathione, its precursors and NAC, because cancer cells can use glutathione to protect themselves from treatment. A large SWOG study also linked antioxidant supplements during chemo with worse outcomes in breast cancer.

Should I tell my oncologist I am doing integrative treatment?

Yes. Dr. Wimbs reminds people that their doctors work for them and that they make the final call. Some oncologists will not endorse outside therapies, but sharing what you take is how a team catches interactions between supplements and cancer drugs.
