Key Takeaways
  • The lymphatic system is a network of tiny vessels and nodes. John calls it a one way river with no pump of its own.
  • Lymph moves when you move, breathe deeply and bend. The lymph nodes sit in the creases of the body, where movement squeezes them.
  • Posture matters. A slight slouch puts pressure on the chest and abdomen, where lymph has to flow up to the neck.
  • Lymph drains back into the blood through small vessels just above the collarbones, which is why John always starts at the neck.
  • Signs John looks for: bloating, swelling, puffiness, swollen legs and ankles, and tightness around the shoulders and neck.
  • Leg fluid has a long way to travel up to the neck. Less movement as we age makes that harder.
  • The LymphMan Shoulder Shuffle: sit tall, raise one shoulder as the other drops, slowly at first, then quickly 20 to 30 times.
  • Deep belly breathing creates pressure changes that help lymph move. Breathe in through the nose and blow out hard.
  • Gentle bouncing on a rebounder helps push lymph up. Sitting on a rebounder or an exercise ball is an option for sore knees or balance.
  • Move a few times a day: stretch your shoulders in the morning, move after lunch, and do the shuffle and deep breaths before bed.
  • John uses small diet changes rather than perfection: more vegetables, less sugar and white flour, a few cleaner days a week.
  • Overdoing it is the most common mistake. John's rule: drain once, then wait a day or two.
Questions Answered

What is the lymphatic system?

The lymphatic system is a network of tiny vessels and nodes that collects fluid, waste and fats from your tissues and moves immune cells through the body. On Dialed In Health, LymphMan John Ossipinsky describes it as a one way river. Unlike blood, which the heart pumps, lymph depends on movement and breathing to flow.

What makes the lymphatic system move if it has no pump?

You do. John Ossipinsky explains that muscle movement, deep breathing and bending squeeze the lymph nodes, which sit in the creases of the body. Walking, stretching your arms, deep belly breaths and even gentle bouncing push lymph along. Long days sitting and a slouched posture slow it down.

How do I know if my lymphatic system is sluggish?

In John Ossipinsky's experience, common signs are bloating, puffiness, swollen ankles or legs, and tightness around the shoulders and neck. He notes it often shows up as fluid building in the body. Swelling that is new, painful, warm or in one leg should be checked by a medical provider before anything else.

How do you do the LymphMan Shoulder Shuffle?

Sit up straight, hands on your lap. Raise one shoulder as the other drops, then switch, slowly at first. With no neck or shoulder pain, speed up and do it 20 to 30 times. John Ossipinsky does it a few times a day and before bed. Check with your doctor first if you have neck or shoulder pain or past surgery.

How does deep breathing help lymph flow?

Deep breathing changes the pressure inside your chest, which helps pull lymph upward. John Ossipinsky teaches belly breathing: hands just below the rib cage, breathe in deeply through the nose and let the belly come out, then blow out hard, like blowing out a birthday candle. He pairs it with the Shoulder Shuffle.

Is a rebounder good for your lymphatic system?

John Ossipinsky uses a rebounder because gentle bouncing helps push lymph upward as the valves in larger lymph vessels open and close. You do not need a hard workout, just a gentle bounce for one to twenty minutes. People with disc or back problems should be careful, and sitting on a rebounder or exercise ball is a gentler option.

Why do ankles and legs swell as we get older?

Lymph from the legs has a long path up through the hips, abdomen and chest to the neck. John Ossipinsky explains that when we move less, muscles squeeze less lymph upward, so fluid can build in the legs. He sees leg exercise and upper body stretching as key. New or one sided swelling should be checked by a provider.

How often should I move my lymphatic system each day?

John Ossipinsky recommends several short moments a day rather than one workout: shoulder stretches and the Shoulder Shuffle in the morning, some movement after lunch, and the shuffle plus deep breaths before bed. He also warns against overdoing it. If you do a full drainage routine, wait a day or two before repeating it.
